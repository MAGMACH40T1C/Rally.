# Project brief: Rally.
## The competition
- I’m entering the Presidential SI Challenge (Standard Track), a national K–12 competition. My age group: Middle school (grades 6–8)
- Judges score only a written entry (a PDF of up to 10 pages) and a video of up to 4 minutes. They reward a clear understanding of the problem and a sensible use of AI.
- My deadlines: 12/18 for registering and 12/23 for submission of project.
## The problem
- People in middle and high school struggle with getting volunteer hours because they can’t find activities they like to do and participate in
- Impact area: School and learning
- Why it matters (impact): These people would be able to find things they like to do in exchange for hours they will need to graduate. They will be able to do something that they actually enjoy instead of waiting for the hours go by without much progress.
- Evidence it’s real: Fact 1: Most teens don’t volunteer through an organization
The fact: AmeriCorps (a U.S. government agency) works with the Census Bureau on a big volunteering survey. In its 2023 numbers, people ages 16-17 had the highest formal volunteering rate at 34.1%. “Formal” means volunteering through a group like a food bank or club. prnewswire
What it means for you: Teens volunteer more than any other age group, but about 2 out of 3 still didn’t volunteer through a group. That leaves a lot of room to help.
Website: PR Newswire, which posted AmeriCorps’ official news release
Link: https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/americorps-the-state-of-volunteering-and-civic-life-in-america-302315771.html
Tip: For more information, the release points to www.AmeriCorps.gov/volunteeringinamerica. That’s the government site itself, so it’s a good one to cite as well.
- What people told me: My friend said that they need volunteer hours.
## My solution
- My AI’s job: My AI helps by finding students somewhere they could earn volunteer hours to graduate or to do something that they actually like, which works better than the student just trying out everything because that is very time consuming and the student could never really know what they actually want to do.
- Tools that already exist, and how mine is different: Many volunteering apps/websites have places where people can browse and find volunteer places, but my app will be able to find volunteer areas for the user and these places will already be chosen just for you interests.
- Safety plan: Risk: Could send a kid somewhere unsafe.

Plan: The app/web will only suggest trusted volunteer areas, show the volunteer area's minimum age, and tell the kid to make sure a trusted adult is always there.

Risk: Kids type personal info.

Plan: Don't ask for names or addresses, don't save chats, and don't make public profiles.
## My MVP features (build these first, in this order)
1. Kids will be able to find volunteer places they want to go to and actually enjoy.
2. The user can see a list of nearby trusted volunteer areas.
3. The user can read descriptions of each volunteer opportunity.
4. The user can access safety tips for volunteering with adults.
5. The AI gives correct, safe answers to real questions.
## Maybe later (not yet)
- The user can filter volunteer opportunities by age requirements.
- The user can view the location of volunteer sites on a map.
- The user can check the schedule and availability of each opportunity.
- The user can save their favorite volunteer opportunities for later.
- The user can share volunteer opportunities with friends via a link.
- The user can report any unsafe or suspicious volunteer listings.
- The user can receive reminders about upcoming volunteer events they signed up for.
- The user can see ratings and reviews for each volunteer organization.
- The user can search for volunteer opportunities by date or timeframe.
- The user can easily reset their preferences for finding new opportunities.
## My AI’s instructions
- When it answers: The user will be able to click a button and be able to go through a checklist.
- Its job: The AI will be able to find volunteering places for the user around them, and according to the checklist, a volunteering place that the user will actually enjoy.
- Who it talks to and how to sound: My AI is talking to almost any student, but mostly from ages 11-17 (Grade6-12)
- Facts it must use: The facts my AI MUST use are the facts from the checklist the user has done prior.
- Rules it must follow: My AI MUST never ask for the user's name (only nickname), address, and anything too personal.
- How to answer: The answer should show a list of volunteering places around the user, answering always in a nice way. The first things that should pop up is a message that's something like "Nice choices!" followed by the volunteer options.
